## Part A: Expert Interview Protocol

### Interview Objectives

Semi-structured interviews with 8–10 expert stakeholders will address research questions that survey data cannot answer:
1. **Jurisprudential Decision-Making:** How do Islamic authorities weigh different maqasid objectives in cryptocurrency classification?
2. **Regulatory Implementation:** What mechanisms do regulators use to operationalize Islamic jurisprudential requirements?
3. **Market Barriers:** What adoption barriers prevent institutional investors from participating in Shariah-compliant cryptocurrencies?
4. **Future Development:** What regulatory innovations and technological advances would enable broader Islamic cryptocurrency adoption?

---

### Interview Participant Categories & Sampling

**Target: 8–10 participants across three expert categories**

**Category A: Regulatory Officials (Target: 3 participants)**
- **Institutions:** OJK (Indonesia), BNM (Malaysia), VARA (UAE)
- **Roles:** Digital Asset Division heads, Fintech Regulatory Leads, Islamic Finance Coordinators
- **Selection Rationale:** Direct insight into regulatory decision-making, implementation constraints, inter-jurisdictional coordination
- **Recruitment:** Formal request through institutional channels; 2-week response window

**Category B: Islamic Scholars & Jurisprudential Experts (Target: 3 participants)**
- **Institutions:** DSN-MUI (Indonesia), Shariah Advisory Boards (Malaysia/UAE), Islamic Development Bank
- **Roles:** Shariah Board members, Islamic Finance Advisory Council participants, Fatwa committee members
- **Selection Rationale:** Direct articulation of Maqasid Shariah application; jurisprudential reasoning
- **Recruitment:** Referral through institutional connections; emphasis on English fluency

**Category C: Cryptocurrency Industry & Technology (Target: 2–4 participants)**
- **Institutions:** AFSI (Islamic Fintech Association), cryptocurrency exchanges with Shariah screening, blockchain/tokenization platforms
- **Roles:** Compliance officers, Shariah board advisors, product development leads
- **Selection Rationale:** Market perspective on compliance requirements, implementation feasibility, customer adoption drivers
- **Recruitment:** Direct outreach through industry associations

---

### Interview Guide Structure

**Duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Format:** Video conference (Zoom/Teams) or in-person  
**Recording:** Audio recording + note-taking (consent obtained on informed consent form)  
**Transcription:** Professional transcription service; participant review option (14-day window)

---

#### Section 1: Background & Institutional Context (5–7 minutes)

**Purpose:** Establish participant expertise and institutional perspective

**Questions:**

1. **Expertise Foundation**
   - "Can you describe your role at [institution] and your background in Islamic finance/cryptocurrency regulation?"
   - "How many years have you been involved in cryptocurrency policy/jurisprudential assessment?"

2. **Institutional Mandate**
   - "What is [institution]'s official position on cryptocurrency and digital assets?"
   - "Who within [institution] drives cryptocurrency policy decisions?"

3. **Stakeholder Interactions**
   - "Which other institutions do you regularly coordinate with on cryptocurrency issues?"
   - "Have you observed changes in coordin­ation intensity in the past 12 months?"

---

#### Section 2: Classification & Jurisprudential Framework (12–15 minutes)

**Purpose:** Understand how experts apply Islamic jurisprudence to specific cryptocurrency characteristics

**Questions for Scholars:**

1. **Maqasid Shariah Application**
   - "When your Shariah board assesses a new cryptocurrency or digital asset, which maqasid do you prioritize? How do you weigh competing objectives (e.g., financial inclusion vs. price stability)?"
   - "Have you observed changes in how different Islamic authorities weight maqasid over time?"
   - "For stablecoins and asset-backed tokens, which maqasid are most satisfied? Which remain problematic?"

2. **Jurisprudential Disagreement**
   - "We observe divergence between Dar al-Ifta (categorical prohibition) and AAOIFI/Taqi Usmani (conditional acceptance) on cryptocurrency. How do you explain this divergence?"
   - "Do you view this as fundamental disagreement or as different factual assessments of cryptocurrency characteristics?"
   - "Is there movement toward consensus among Islamic authorities?"

3. **Innovation Accommodation**
   - "Do Islamic jurisprudential principles enable accommodation of technological innovation, or are they primarily restrictive?"
   - "What specific architectural changes (asset backing, governance structure, utility design) would move a cryptocurrency from 'prohibited' to 'conditionally acceptable' category?"

**Questions for Regulators:**

1. **Regulatory Operationalization**
   - "How does [institution] translate Islamic jurisprudential principles into concrete regulatory requirements?"
   - "In developing regulatory sandbox models for digital assets, which Shariah compliance criteria are non-negotiable?"
   - "How do you verify that a digital asset meets Shariah requirements before regulatory approval?"

2. **Coordination with Scholars**
   - "Does [institution] consult with Islamic scholars during regulatory decision-making? If so, how is this coordination structured?"
   - "Have regulatory and jurisprudential positions evolved together, or independently?"

**Questions for Industry:**

1. **Compliance Pathway**
   - "From a technology developer perspective, what are the primary Shariah compliance barriers for cryptocurrency projects?"
   - "Which compliance requirements are most costly to implement?"
   - "Are there regulatory shortcuts or 'checkbox compliance' approaches, or is Shariah compliance genuinely integrated into technology design?"

2. **Institutional Adoption**
   - "What prevents Islamic financial institutions from participating in Shariah-compliant cryptocurrencies?"
   - "Are barriers primarily regulatory, Shariah-related, or operational/technical?"

---

#### Section 3: Regulatory Implementation & Sandbox Experience (12–15 minutes)

**Purpose:** Understand practical implementation of classification frameworks

**Questions for Regulators:**

1. **Sandbox Design Philosophy**
   - "What was the rationale for designing seven specific sandbox models rather than a single universal approval pathway?"
   - "How does each sandbox model address different Shariah compliance dimensions?"

2. **GIDR Case Study**
   - "Walk me through OJK's decision process for approving GIDR tokenization in the sandbox."
   - "What specific evidence or criteria satisfied the asset-backing requirement?"
   - "Did the DSN-MUI coordinate on GIDR approval? If so, what was the nature of that coordination?"

3. **Regulatory Learning**
   - "What did sandbox testing reveal about market demand for Shariah-compliant digital assets?"
   - "Did any sandbox models reveal unexpected operational or Shariah compliance challenges?"
   - "How are sandbox findings informing full-market regulatory policy?"

**Questions for Industry:**

1. **Sandbox Participation**
   - "Is [your organization] participating in the regulatory sandbox? If so, which models?"
   - "What has sandbox participation revealed about customer demand for Shariah-compliant assets?"
   - "What regulatory or Shariah compliance requirements were unexpected or challenging?"

2. **Market Transition**
   - "When sandbox models move to full-market approval, what changes in institutional adoption do you expect?"
   - "Will different types of institutions (banks vs. funds vs. trading firms) participate differently?"

---

#### Section 4: Adoption Barriers & Institutional Participation (8–10 minutes)

**Purpose:** Understand what prevents broader adoption of Shariah-compliant digital assets

**Questions for All Categories:**

1. **Investor Hesitation**
   - "From your perspective, why haven't institutional investors more broadly adopted Shariah-compliant cryptocurrencies despite regulatory approval in multiple jurisdictions?"
   - "Is the barrier primarily price volatility, governance concerns, regulatory uncertainty, or Shariah skepticism?"

2. **Cross-Jurisdictional Recognition**
   - "If a digital asset is approved in Malaysia (Category A), will it automatically receive recognition in Indonesia or Saudi Arabia?"
   - "What regulatory harmonization mechanisms would facilitate cross-border institutional participation?"

3. **Retail vs. Institutional**
   - "Do you observe different adoption patterns between retail and institutional investors?"
   - "Are institutional barriers different from retail barriers?"

---

#### Section 5: Future Development & Research Implications (8–10 minutes)

**Purpose:** Identify emerging issues and research opportunities

**Questions for All Categories:**

1. **Emerging Technologies**
   - "What emerging cryptocurrency or blockchain technologies are most likely to raise new Shariah compliance questions in the next 2–3 years? (DeFi protocols, layer-2 scaling, cross-chain bridges, tokenized securities)"
   - "Should Islamic jurisprudence be proactively updated for these emerging technologies or reactively addressed as issues arise?"

2. **Regulatory Coordination**
   - "Would formal inter-Islamic regulatory coordination (e.g., an Inter-Islamic Finance Regulatory Council) improve cryptocurrency regulation in Islamic jurisdictions?"
   - "What would be the barriers to such coordination?"
   - "What would be the benefits?"

3. **Institutional Capacity**
   - "Do Islamic regulators and scholars have adequate capacity (expertise, resources) to keep pace with cryptocurrency innovation?"
   - "What training or institution-building would strengthen Islamic jurisprudential and regulatory capacity?"

4. **Research Priorities**
   - "From your perspective, what aspects of cryptocurrency and Islamic finance are most in need of additional research?"
   - "Are there gaps in our understanding of how Maqasid Shariah applies to digital assets?"

---

#### Section 6: Closing & Clarification (2–3 minutes)

**Purpose:** Capture any additional insights and confirm follow-up protocols

**Questions:**

1. "Is there anything important we haven't discussed that you think is relevant to understanding cryptocurrency and Islamic finance?"
2. "May I follow up if clarification questions emerge during data analysis?"
3. "When findings are compiled, would you like to review a summary before publication?"

### Interview Administration

**Scheduling:**
- Pre-interview confirmation email 1 week before
- Time zone accommodation for international participants
- Recording/transcription consent obtained in informed consent form (sent separately)

**Technical Setup:**
- Platform: Zoom (preferred) or Teams

- Recording settings: Audio + video (with participant permission)
- Backup: Voice memo app on phone if technical failure

## Part B: Survey Data Structure

### Survey Platform & Distribution

**Platform:** Qualtrics (UIN institutional license)  
**Language:** Indonesian (primary), English (secondary)  
**Distribution Channels:**
- Cryptocurrency exchange platforms (opt-in survey links)
- AFTECH/AFSI/ABI member mailing lists
- Social media (Facebook cryptocurrency groups, Reddit r/IndoCrypto)
- Snowball sampling (survey respondents refer peers)

**Target Sample:**
- **Indonesia:** 1,200–1,500 respondents
- **Malaysia:** 500–750 respondents
- **Other Islamic jurisdictions:** 300–500 respondents
- **Total:** 2,000–3,000 respondents

**Survey Duration:** 18–20 minutes  
**Incentive:** 50,000–75,000 IDR per valid response (via e-wallet/pulsa before survey deployment)  
**Target Completion:** August–September 2026 (8-week window)

---

### Survey Data Structure (52 Items)

#### Section A: Demographic & Background Information (7 items)

**Questions:**
- A1: Age (years) — Open response
- A2: Gender — Radio button (Male / Female / Prefer not to answer)
- A3: Education level — Radio button (SMP+ / SMA+ / Diploma / S1 / S2/S3)
- A4: Primary occupation — Radio button (Student / Government / Finance / Private sector / Retired / Unemployed / Other)
- A5: Monthly income — Radio button (Rp0–1M / Rp1–5M / Rp5–10M / Rp10–40M / Rp40M+)
- A6: Years of crypto experience — Radio button (<6mo / 6–12mo / 1–2yr / 2–4yr / >4yr)
- A7: Province of residence — Dropdown (38 Indonesian provinces)

**Data Type:** Categorical/ordinal (coded 1–5 for analysis)  
**Missing Data Handling:** Required questions except A7 (default: "Not specified")

---

#### Section B: Cryptocurrency Knowledge & Behavior (6 items)

**Questions:**
- B1: Primary cryptocurrency holdings — Checkbox multiple (Bitcoin / Ethereum / Solana / USDT / USDC / Token emas [GIDR] / Lainnya)
- B2: Transaction frequency — Radio button (Daily / Weekly / Monthly / Several times/year / Rarely)
- B3: Primary exchange platform — Open text (platform name, coding: regulated vs. unregulated)
- B4: Portfolio composition — Slider 0–100% (percentage of total portfolio in cryptocurrency)
- B5: Investment motivation (choose single most important) — Radio button (Financial gain / Technology interest / Religious compliance / Remittance efficiency / Speculation / Other)
- B6: Reasons affecting investment decisions (Likert 1–5 scale) — Multi-item:
  - Financial returns
  - Technology development
  - Religious/Shariah compliance
  - Regulatory clarity
  - Market volatility/risk

**Data Type:** Mixed (categorical, ordinal, continuous)  
**Reverse Coding:** None (all items scored in same direction)  
**Attention Check:** B4 slider position recorded; extreme/inconsistent values flagged for validation

---

#### Section C: Shariah Compliance Awareness (6 items)

**Questions:**
- C1: Awareness of Shariah status — Radio button (Yes / No)
- C2: Familiarity with Ijtima' Ulama MUI 2021 decision — Radio button (Yes / No)
- C3: Knowledge of OJK regulatory transition (Jan 2025) — Radio button (Yes / No)
- C4: Awareness of asset-backed digital assets (e.g., GIDR) — Radio button (Yes / No)
- C5: Information sources for Shariah status (Likert 1–5: importance) — Multi-item:
  - Social media
  - News/financial media
  - Religious authority recommendations
  - Regulatory websites
  - Trusted peers/family
  - Other
- C6: Importance of Shariah compliance in investment decisions — Likert 1–5 scale

**Data Type:** Categorical (C1–C4), Ordinal (C5–C6)  
**Analysis:** C1–C4 will generate institutional authority gap data (items H4–H5 in regulation quiz validate institutional messaging)

---

#### Section D: Platform & Exchange Trust (6 items)

**Questions:**
- D1: Primary exchange trust level — Likert 1–5 (Very distrustful to Very trustful)
- D2: Exchange regulatory status awareness — Radio button (Licensed by OJK / Licensed by foreign regulator / Unregulated / Don't know)
- D3: Shariah screening availability — Radio button (Yes / No / Don't know)
- D4: Willingness to use only OJK-licensed exchanges — Likert 1–5
- D5: Impact of regulatory clarity on platform choice — Likert 1–5 (Not at all → Very much)
- D6: Customer support quality satisfaction — Likert 1–5

**Data Type:** Ordinal  
**Validation:** D2 response matched against actual exchange licensing status for accuracy assessment

---

#### Section E: Investment Sophistication & Risk Tolerance (6 items)

**Questions:**
- E1: Cryptocurrency price volatility tolerance — Likert 1–5 (Very intolerant to Very tolerant)
- E2: Understanding of cryptocurrency technology — Likert 1–5 (No understanding to Expert level)
- E3: Understanding of asset-backed tokenization — Likert 1–5
- E4: Prior investment experience (stocks/bonds/funds) — Radio button (No / 1–5yr / 5–10yr / >10yr)
- E5: Use of portfolio diversification strategy — Radio button (Yes / No)
- E6: Investment time horizon — Radio button (<1yr / 1–3yr / 3–5yr / >5yr / Not planned)

**Data Type:** Ordinal (Likert), Categorical (radio)  
**Analysis:** E1–E3 combined into "sophistication index" predicting adoption patterns

---

#### Section F: Regulatory Perception & Institutional Trust (5 items)

**Questions:**
- F1: Trust in OJK regulatory oversight — Likert 1–5
- F2: Perceived regulatory clarity on cryptocurrency status — Likert 1–5
- F3: Concern about regulatory crackdown — Likert 1–5 (Not concerned to Very concerned)
- F4: Confidence in Bank Indonesia/OJK coordination — Likert 1–5
- F5: Impact of regulatory approval on investment willingness — Likert 1–5

**Data Type:** Ordinal  
**Analysis:** F1–F5 combined into "regulatory trust index"

---

#### Section G: Barriers & Adoption Drivers (6 items)

**Questions:**
- G1: Most important barrier to broader crypto participation (choose 1) — Radio button (Price volatility / Regulatory uncertainty / Shariah concerns / Technical complexity / Fraud/scam risk / Insufficient financial resources / Other)
- G2: Impact of Shariah compliance clarity on adoption — Likert 1–5
- G3: Impact of GIDR (gold-backed token) on adoption interest — Likert 1–5
- G4: Would participate in OJK-approved Shariah-compliant assets — Likert 1–5
- G5: Barrier importance ratings (Likert 1–5 for each):
  - Price volatility
  - Regulatory uncertainty
  - Shariah compliance doubt
  - Fraud/scam risk
  - Technical complexity
- G6: Feature preferences for Shariah-compliant digital asset (Likert 1–5 for each):
  - Physical asset backing
  - Stable price
  - Utility/productive use
  - Community governance
  - Regulatory approval

**Data Type:** Categorical (G1), Ordinal (G2–G6)  
**Analysis:** G1–G6 map directly to dissertation framework dimensions

---

#### Section H: Regulation & Shariah Knowledge Quiz (8 items) [REVISED]

**Purpose:** Objective assessment of knowledge; validates institutional messaging effectiveness

**Questions (All: True/False or Multiple Choice):**

- H1: **OJK Regulatory Transition** — "In January 2025, oversight of cryptocurrency moved from [Agency A] to OJK." (True/False) [Correct: True]

- H2: **Ijtima' Ulama Decision** — "The 2021 Ijtima' Ulama MUI decided that cryptocurrency is haram as currency but potentially acceptable if [___]." (Multiple choice: a) backed by gold, b) approved by Bappebti, c) traded on regulated exchanges, d) speculative only) [Correct: a) backed by gold — Fatwa MUI Butir 3]

- H3: **Fatwa Scope** — "The 2021 MUI Fatwa on cryptocurrency applies to which of the following: Bitcoin, Ethereum, stablecoins, asset-backed tokens, or all of the above?" (Multiple choice) [Correct: All of the above, with different requirements]

- H4: **Shariah Compliance Authority** — "Who has primary authority to determine Shariah compliance of cryptocurrencies in Indonesia?" (Multiple choice: a) DSN-MUI, b) OJK, c) Both coordinate, d) Individual Shariah board) [Correct: c) Both coordinate]

- H5: **Regulatory Clarity** — "Which institution has published the clearest guidance on which cryptocurrencies meet Islamic requirements?" (Multiple choice: a) OJK, b) DSN-MUI, c) Both equally clear, d) Neither — institutional authority gap] [Correct: d) Neither — validates institutional authority gap finding]

- H6: **GIDR Token** — "GIDR (Gold Indonesia Republic) is designed to be Shariah-compliant by [___]." (Multiple choice: a) having productive use, b) being backed 1:1 by physical gold, c) having decentralized governance, d) being listed on OJK-licensed exchange) [Correct: b) being backed 1:1 by physical gold]

- H7: **Sandbox Models** — "OJK's regulatory sandbox includes [___] models for testing digital assets." (Multiple choice: 5 / 7 / 10 / Don't know) [Correct: 7]

- H8: **Asset-Backed Requirement** — "Islamic jurisprudence requires asset-backed digital assets but permits pure speculative cryptocurrencies." (True/False) [Correct: False — Islamic positions prohibit speculative cryptocurrencies]

**Scoring:**
- Correct = 1 point, Incorrect = 0 points
- Total knowledge score: 0–8
- Knowledge categories: 0–2 (Low), 3–5 (Moderate), 6–8 (High)

**Data Type:** Binary (correct/incorrect), analyzed as count score  
**Validation:** H4–H5 responses cross-tabulated with C1–C5 to assess information source effectiveness

---

#### Screening Questions (3 items, administered first)

**Purpose:** Confirm eligibility; enable skip logic

- S1: **Crypto Experience** — "Have you owned or traded cryptocurrency in the past 12 months?" (Yes → continue / No → end survey with thanks)

- S2: **Residency** — "Are you currently based in Indonesia or Malaysia?" (Yes → continue / No → optional end survey or continue for regional comparison)

- S3: **Age & Consent** — "Are you age 18 or older and willing to participate in this research?" (Yes → continue to informed consent / No → end survey)

**Data Type:** Binary (Yes/No)  
**Analysis:** S1 responses determine sample composition; S2 enables country-level analysis

### Survey Data Management

**Data Storage:**
- Qualtrics servers (encrypted, UIN institutional backup)
- Responses downloaded weekly in CSV format
- Data retained for 3 years per UIN IRB protocol; destroyed after

**Data Cleaning Protocol:**
- Duplicate IP addresses flagged (remove if identical response timing <2min)
- Survey completion time <8min flagged as potential speeding; review case-by-case
- Missing data pattern review (>20% missing → exclude case)
- Attention check (B4 slider) validation: extreme/inconsistent → flag but retain
- Outlier analysis: income/age values >3SD from mean flagged for review

**Variable Coding:**
- A1–A7: Demographic variables coded 1–5 or numeric
- B1–B6: Behavior variables coded as factor/numeric with labeling
- C1–C6: Awareness/Shariah variables coded as factor (Yes/No/Don't know)
- D1–D6: Trust/platform variables coded 1–5 (Likert)
- E1–E6: Sophistication variables coded 1–5 + categorical
- F1–F5: Regulatory trust coded 1–5
- G1–G6: Barriers/drivers coded 1–5
- H1–H8: Knowledge quiz coded 1–0 (correct/incorrect), summed to 0–8 score
- S1–S3: Screening coded Yes/No

**Analysis Software:** R (tidyverse, ggplot2) or SPSS for descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, factor analysis

## Part D: Data Analysis Plan

### Qualitative Analysis (Expert Interviews)

**Approach:** Thematic analysis using NVivo or Atlas.ti

**Themes to Code:**
1. **Maqasid Application:** How do experts weigh different Islamic objectives in cryptocurrency assessment?
2. **Regulatory Implementation:** What operational mechanisms translate jurisprudence to policy?
3. **Adoption Barriers:** What prevents institutional participation?
4. **Institutional Coordination:** What coordination mechanisms exist/are needed?
5. **Future Direction:** What emerging issues require jurisprudential/regulatory attention?

**Output:** Thematic summary document with representative quotations; framework validation/refinement

### Quantitative Analysis (Survey Data)

**Descriptive Statistics:**
- Demographic profile comparison (Indonesia vs. Malaysia)
- Adoption rates by asset type (Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT, GIDR)
- Knowledge scores distribution (H1–H8 average and by demographic groups)
- Regulatory trust index by group

**Bivariate Analysis:**
- Correlation: Shariah awareness (C1–C4) → Investment behavior (G4)
- Correlation: Knowledge scores (H) → Platform trust (D1)
- Chi-square: Knowledge level (H score category) vs. Income/education
- T-test: Trust levels (F index) between respondents aware vs. unaware of MUI Fatwa

**Multivariate Analysis:**
- Factor analysis: Collapse C1–C6 into "Shariah awareness latent variable"
- Factor analysis: Collapse F1–F5 into "Regulatory trust latent variable"
- Factor analysis: Collapse G5–G6 into "Barrier importance latent variables"
- Regression: Predict willingness to participate (G4) from knowledge (H), awareness (C), trust (F), sophistication (E)
- Mediation analysis: Does regulatory clarity (F2) mediate the relationship between knowledge (H) and adoption intent (G4)?

**Institutional Authority Gap Analysis:**
- Compare responses to H4 (coordinated authority) vs. H5 (no clear authority)
- Cross-tabulate with C1–C5 (information sources) to identify messaging gaps
- Identify which information sources yield more accurate institutional understanding

**Output:** Statistical summary tables, regression results, visualization plots (ggplot2), findings report

## Part E: Ethical Protocols

### Informed Consent
- Informed consent form distributed before survey/interview
- Survey: Checkbox required ("I consent to participate")
- Interview: Verbal consent recorded at session start, documented in transcript

### Privacy & Confidentiality
- Survey responses: Identifiers not collected (optional email for incentive only)
- Interviews: Participant names removed before transcription (coded as "Regulator A," "Scholar B," etc.)
- All data uploaded to encrypted UIN server; no local copies stored
- Audio recordings and transcripts deleted after 3 years per IRB protocol

### Data Security
- Qualtrics data exported only to password-protected computers
- Interview recordings stored in encrypted folder (AES-256)
- Access limited to research team (researcher + advisor)
- No third-party vendors access raw data

### Participant Compensation
- Incentive: 50,000–75,000 IDR (distributed via e-wallet/pulsa before survey)
- No contingency on completion quality; all respondents receive incentive upon validation
- Interview participants offered findings summary (optional)
