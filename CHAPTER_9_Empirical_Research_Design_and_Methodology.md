# Chapter 9: Empirical Research Design and Methodology

## Introduction: From Theory to Evidence

Chapters 1 through 8 have established a comprehensive theoretical framework for cryptocurrency classification under Islamic law, including a five-dimensional assessment model (Maqasid Shariah-grounded), institutional coordination mechanism (IIFRC), and research agenda for empirical validation. This chapter documents the empirical research design through which the theoretical framework is operationalized, tested, and refined through systematic field research in Islamic financial markets.

The empirical research is structured as a mixed-methods study combining quantitative survey research, qualitative interviews, and institutional case analysis. The research is conducted across the five major Islamic jurisdictions analyzed in the dissertation (Indonesia, Malaysia, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Bahrain), with particular focus on Indonesia as primary research site during initial phases.

---

## Research Questions and Hypotheses

The empirical research addresses six core questions operationalizing the theoretical framework:

**RQ1 (Dimensional Validity):** Do the five dimensions of the classification framework (asset backing, value stability, productive utility, governance/decentralization, regulatory recognition) predict meaningful variance in institutional adoption behavior and investment preferences among Islamic financial institutions?

**RQ2 (Framework Comprehensiveness):** Do market participants perceive the five-dimensional framework as capturing the salient attributes determining Islamic compliance assessment, or are important considerations omitted?

**RQ3 (Measurement Consistency):** Can the five-dimensional scoring methodology be reliably applied across different raters, institutions, and jurisdictions, or does subjective interpretation prevent standardization?

**RQ4 (Jurisprudential Alignment):** Do Islamic scholars' assessments of cryptocurrency compliance align with the five-dimensional framework scoring, or do important jurisprudential considerations fall outside the framework?

**RQ5 (Regulatory Feasibility):** Can the IIFRC coordination model function across diverse Islamic jurisdictions with different regulatory philosophies, or do political economy and institutional factors prevent effective coordination?

**RQ6 (Market Response):** Do cryptocurrency projects with higher Islamic compliance scores (by the framework) achieve higher institutional adoption and faster regulatory approval?

## Mixed-Methods Research Design

### Phase 1A: Quantitative Survey Research

**Population and Sampling:**

The survey targets 500+ respondents distributed across four stakeholder groups with distinct perspectives on cryptocurrency and Islamic compliance:

1. **Islamic Financial Institution Professionals (n=150):** Senior staff at Islamic banks, Islamic asset managers, Islamic wealth managers, and institutional Shariah boards across five major jurisdictions. Sampling through: regulatory authority referrals, industry association lists (Association of Islamic Banking Institutions Indonesia, Malaysian Islamic Banks Association, equivalent bodies in Saudi Arabia/UAE/Bahrain), direct institutional outreach.

2. **Cryptocurrency Exchange Operators and Staff (n=100):** Technical, compliance, and strategy staff at major cryptocurrency exchanges with regional presence (Tokocrypto, Luno, Binance regional operations, Coinbase regional presence, etc.). Sampling through direct institutional outreach and industry networking.

3. **Cryptocurrency Project Teams (n=100):** Founders, compliance officers, and strategy leads at cryptocurrency and blockchain projects targeting Islamic markets. Sampling through industry directories, conferences, and direct outreach.

4. **Retail Cryptocurrency Investors (n=150):** Adults (18+) with active cryptocurrency investment in past 12 months, recruited through online panels, cryptocurrency communities, and Islamic finance forums. Oversampling from Indonesia and Malaysia to reflect primary research context.

**Survey Instrument:**

The survey is administered online via Qualtrics and takes approximately 45-60 minutes to complete. The instrument comprises seven sections:

**Section A: Demographics and Background (5 minutes)**
- Age, education, country of residence
- Years of cryptocurrency experience
- Familiarity with Islamic finance (1-5 Likert scale)
- Current role and institutional affiliation

**Section B: Investment Motivations and Values (5 minutes)**
- Reasons for cryptocurrency participation (multiple selection: returns, Islamic compliance, technology interest, financial inclusion, speculation, other)
- Importance ratings (1-5 Likert) for: investment returns, Islamic compliance, price stability, practical utility, transparent governance, regulatory recognition

**Section C: Dimensional Perception Study (8 minutes)**
- Introduction to five-dimensional framework with detailed explanations
- Importance rating for each dimension (1-5 Likert scale)
- Conjoint analysis with 10 choice scenarios testing attribute trade-offs
- Forced allocation task: distribute 100 points across five dimensions reflecting relative weight

**Section D: Framework Validation (6 minutes)**
- Overall utility rating of five-dimensional framework (1-5 scale)
- Open-ended question on omitted attributes or concerns
- Likelihood of using framework for investment/product decisions

**Section E: Case Study Assessment (6 minutes)**
Respondents evaluate five standardized cryptocurrency descriptions:
- Bitcoin: High utility, zero backing, high volatility, decentralized governance, weak Islamic recognition
- Ethereum: Moderate utility, no backing, high volatility, distributed governance, no Islamic recognition
- USDC: High backing, limited utility, low volatility, centralized governance, moderate Islamic recognition
- Hypothetical Islamic Stablecoin: Full backing, emerging utility, low volatility, Islamic governance
- Hypothetical Islamic Digital Asset: Asset-backed, specialized utility, low volatility, Islamic governance

For each, rate: investment attractiveness (1-5), perceived Islamic compliance (1-5), expected institutional adoption likelihood (1-5)

**Section F: Disclosure and Trust Preferences (4 minutes)**
- Information needed for Islamic compliance assessment (open-ended)
- Importance of various disclosures: reserve verification, governance documentation, Shariah board composition, regulatory approval, third-party audits
- Trust ranking: project self-disclosure, exchange verification, independent auditor, Shariah board, regulatory authority

**Section G: Regulatory/Institutional-Specific Questions (4 minutes)**
- For institutional respondents: regulatory barriers and enablers for cryptocurrency investment
- For project respondents: barriers to Islamic compliance achievement
- For exchange respondents: support needed for Islamic cryptocurrency products

**Data Quality Assurance:**

- Attention check item (Section A): "Select 'Setuju' if you are reading carefully"
- Duration monitoring: responses <300 seconds (5 minutes) flagged as rushed
- Straight-lining detection: respondents selecting identical values across entire scales excluded
- Consistency checks: contradictory responses (e.g., high Islamic importance but zero Islamic knowledge) flagged for review

**Target Completion Timeline:**
- Survey deployment: Week 6 (August 8-15, 2026)
- Data collection window: 4-6 weeks
- Target: 500+ completed responses by Week 11 (September 15, 2026)

### Phase 1B: Qualitative Interview Research

**Sample and Recruitment:**

In parallel with survey administration, qualitative interviews (60-90 minutes each) with 30-40 purposefully selected respondents provide in-depth exploration of cryptocurrency adoption decision-making, institutional barriers, and IIFRC feasibility assessment.

Interview participants represent diverse perspectives:
- **10 institutional Islamic finance decision-makers:** Senior investment committee members, risk officers, Shariah board chairs at Islamic banks/asset managers
- **10 cryptocurrency project leaders:** Founders, compliance officers, strategy leads discussing product design and regulatory engagement
- **10 retail investors with stated Islamic compliance priorities:** Investors whose survey responses indicate Islamic compliance is primary decision criterion
- **10 market observers:** Industry analysts, regulators, consultants providing external perspective on market dynamics and institutional constraints

**Interview Protocol:**

Semi-structured interview guide (12-15 open-ended questions) exploring:

1. **Adoption Decision-Making:** "Walk me through a recent cryptocurrency investment decision. What factors were most important? How did Islamic compliance fit into your analysis?"

2. **Information Needs:** "What information would you need to confidently assess Islamic compliance of a cryptocurrency?"

3. **Institutional Barriers:** "What barriers prevent your institution from greater cryptocurrency participation? Which barriers are regulatory, technical, knowledge-based, risk-management-based?"

4. **Framework Assessment:** "How well does the five-dimensional classification framework capture what's important for your decision-making? What's missing?"

5. **Regulatory Coordination:** "Is cross-jurisdictional regulatory coordination (IIFRC model) realistic given current institutional dynamics? What would enable or prevent it?"

6. **Market Evolution:** "How do you see the Islamic cryptocurrency market evolving over the next 2-3 years? What role do you expect your institution to play?"

**Data Management:**
- All interviews audio-recorded (with consent) and professionally transcribed
- Transcripts coded using thematic analysis software (NVivo or ATLAS.ti)
- Thematic analysis focusing on: adoption enablers/barriers, information asymmetries, regulatory concerns, framework validation

### Phase 2: Institutional Data Access and Exchange Requests

**Data Exchange Protocol:**

Systematic requests to major cryptocurrency platforms (Tokocrypto, Luno, Binance regional operations, other exchanges serving Islamic markets) for anonymized aggregate data on:

1. **Islamic Institutional Holdings:** Monthly aggregate holdings of cryptocurrency by Islamic financial institutions (banks, asset managers), disaggregated by cryptocurrency and institution type

2. **Cryptocurrency Characteristics:** Verified asset-backing levels, governance structure documentation, regulatory approval status across jurisdictions

3. **Adoption Trends:** New institutional customer acquisition rates, product adoption metrics (custody, trading, lending services), retention rates

4. **Compliance Infrastructure:** How exchanges assess Islamic compliance, what disclosures they require, integration with Shariah board guidance

**Data Sharing Framework:**

All data exchange requests include:
- Explicit commitment to anonymization and privacy (UU Perlindungan Data Pribadi compliance)
- Right for exchange to review factual claims before publication (review right ≠ editorial veto)
- Benefit-sharing: exchange receives summary findings about Islamic institutional adoption trends
- Publication timeline coordination (minimum 30-day advance notice before publication)

**Timeline:**
- Data exchange requests submitted: Week 6 (August 8-15, 2026)
- Expected response and negotiation: 4-6 weeks
- Data access: projected September-October 2026

## Measurement Instruments and Validation

### Scale Construction and Reliability Targets

The survey includes eight multi-item scales measuring constructs from the theoretical framework and Islamic finance adoption literature:

**Scale D: Awareness of Shariah Concerns** (5 items)
- Item examples: "Pure cryptocurrencies create uncertainty (gharar) incompatible with Islamic transactions," "Asset-backed digital assets better align with Islamic finance principles"
- Cronbach's α target: 0.75
- Rationale: Measures awareness of Islamic jurisprudential concerns grounding the framework

**Scale E: Perceived Usefulness** (5 items)
- Item examples: "Cryptocurrency enables financial inclusion," "Digital assets support remittance efficiency"
- Cronbach's α target: 0.70
- Rationale: Measures perceived utility dimension of framework

**Scale F: Ease/Barriers** (5 items, reverse-coded items)
- Item examples: "Regulatory uncertainty hinders institutional participation," "Technical complexity prevents adoption"
- Cronbach's α target: 0.70
- Rationale: Measures institutional barriers to adoption

**Scale G: Trust in Regulators** (5 items, some reverse-coded)
- Item examples: "Islamic regulators are trustworthy sources of compliance guidance," "Regulatory coordination would improve market outcomes"
- Cronbach's α target: 0.70
- Rationale: Measures confidence in regulatory assessment and coordination

**Scale I: Social Norms** (3 items)
- Item examples: "Important peers in my institution view cryptocurrency positively," "Professional colleagues recommend Islamic-compliant crypto exposure"
- Cronbach's α target: 0.65
- Rationale: Measures social influence on adoption intentions

**Scale J: Perceived Behavioral Control** (3 items, one reverse-coded)
- Item examples: "My institution has capacity to manage cryptocurrency risks," "I have sufficient knowledge to evaluate compliance"
- Cronbach's α target: 0.65
- Rationale: Measures self-efficacy for cryptocurrency adoption

**Scale K: Risk Aversion** (4 items)
- Item examples: "Cryptocurrency volatility is excessive," "Unproven assets should not be institutional investments"
- Cronbach's α target: 0.70
- Rationale: Measures risk tolerance affecting adoption

**Scale L: Crypto/Islamic Finance Literacy** (5 items including knowledge questions)
- Item examples: MCQ on blockchain technology, Islamic jurisprudential positions on cryptocurrency, regulatory frameworks
- Target: >60% correct responses across cohort
- Rationale: Measures domain expertise predicting investment sophistication

### Knowledge Questions (Kuis Pengetahuan)

Five multiple-choice questions assessing knowledge of regulatory and jurisprudential landscape:

- **H1:** Which Islamic authority issued the most recent fatwa on cryptocurrency permissibility? (Options: Saudi GSIA, Indonesia MUI, Malaysia BNM, Dar al-Ifta Egypt)
- **H2:** What was the OJK's primary regulatory approach to cryptocurrency? (Options: direct prohibition, commodity classification, licensing framework, open sandbox)
- **H3:** Which dimension is NOT part of the five-dimensional classification framework? (Options: asset backing, volatility, utility, governance, market cap)
- **H4:** What is the primary goal of IIFRC coordination? (Options: enforce uniform regulation, establish minimum standards with local flexibility, harmonize all regulatory approaches, eliminate cryptocurrency)
- **H5:** Which cryptocurrency characteristic most reduces Islamic compliance risk? (Options: decentralization, volatility, asset backing, brand recognition)

**Scoring:** Correct response = 1 point; incorrect = 0. Target: ≥60% mean correctness across respondents indicates adequate baseline knowledge.

## Analytical Approach and Statistical Methods

### Quantitative Analysis

**Descriptive Statistics:**
- Demographic profile of respondents by group (institutional, exchange, project, retail)
- Distribution of scale scores and dimensional importance ratings
- Comparison of mean scores across respondent groups

**Reliability and Validity:**
- Cronbach's α for each scale; items with item-total correlation <0.30 flagged for revision
- Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) to verify dimensional structure of multi-item scales
- Convergent and discriminant validity assessment

**Primary Analysis:**
- Conjoint analysis using conditional logit regression to estimate implicit preference weights for cryptocurrency attributes
- Multiple regression: outcome variables (institutional adoption intention, investment attractiveness, perceived compliance) predicted by framework scores, respondent characteristics, knowledge
- Moderation analysis: test whether institutional type (bank vs. asset manager) moderates relationships between framework scores and adoption intention

**Market Outcome Validation:**
- Correlation between framework scores and actual institutional holdings data (from Phase 2 data exchange)
- Regression discontinuity design: test whether cryptocurrencies crossing Category A threshold show discontinuous increase in institutional adoption

### Qualitative Analysis

- Thematic coding of interview transcripts using NVivo/ATLAS.ti
- Identification of adoption enablers, barriers, and framework validation themes
- Framework refinement: compilation of suggested dimensional modifications or missing attributes
- Institutional feasibility assessment: analysis of IIFRC coordination challenges and opportunities

## Ethical Considerations and Approvals
