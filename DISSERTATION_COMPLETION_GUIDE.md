### Chapter 2 – Islamic Legal Framework (~4,500 words)

**Status:** 🟡 Core content solid. Two sections need strengthening.

#### Gap 1: Missing counterarguments (WRITE THIS FIRST in Ch2)

The Maqasid analysis in §2.1–2.5 scores Bitcoin 2/5, arriving at a predominantly negative conclusion. The chapter currently has **minimal engagement with the pro-permissibility scholarly perspective**. An examiner will notice this.

**What to write:** A new §2.6 "Counterarguments and the Case for Conditional Permissibility" (~1,200 words):
- Scholars who argue cryptocurrency *serves* Maqasid Shariah goals, especially Al-Karamah (dignity/financial inclusion for unbanked Muslims) and Al-Maslaha (public benefit of reducing transaction costs)
- The argument that prohibition of beneficial instruments requires *stronger* evidence than permission (the baseline is permissibility in Islamic commercial law)
- How stablecoins and commodity-backed tokens might satisfy maqasid criteria that pure crypto fails

**Key sources to cite:**
- Muhammed Al-Bashir Al-Amine, *Cryptocurrency and Islamic Finance* (2020) — permissibility argument
- Mohd Daud Bakar, various *fatwas* on digital assets in Malaysia (2019–2022) — conditional permissibility
- Habib Ahmed, "Islamic finance in the digital era" (2019) — financial inclusion and maqasid
- Jasser Auda, *Maqasid Al-Shari'ah as Philosophy of Islamic Law* (2008) — methodology for weighing competing maqasid

#### Gap 2: Fiqh school variations not addressed (~800 words)

The chapter treats "Islamic law" monolithically. Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali schools have meaningfully different approaches to novel property (mal jadid) and gharar (uncertainty). Bahrain's CBB follows one jurisprudential tradition; Malaysia's BNM another.

**What to write:** A §2.7 "Jurisprudential School Variations" section (~800 words):
- How Hanafi school's broader definition of mal (property) affects cryptocurrency classification
- Maliki school's role of 'urf (custom) in validating new exchange instruments
- Why MUI Indonesia's position differs from AAOIFI (different fiqh methodologies)

**Key sources:**
- Al-Zuhayli, Wahbah. *Islamic Jurisprudence and Its Proofs* (2003) — fiqh school approaches to mal
- Kamali, Mohammad Hashim. *Principles of Islamic Jurisprudence* (4th ed., 2019) — essential reference
- OJK and MUI joint ruling on crypto (2021) — Indonesian Shafi'i-influenced methodology
